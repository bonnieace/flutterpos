Calls to action

The primary is ink, not green — #15251B on white. A saturated green button on a white page reads consumer; a near‑black one reads like software somebody pays for, which is the register the brief asks for. Green stays for kickers, ticks and the checked‑day status, so it still signals the brand without carrying the click. On the closing dark band the accent comes back, because there the contrast runs the other way.

The one field on the page

Both CTAs lead to the same thing: an email, then a password. It matters here because the page’s whole promise is that it tells you the truth early, and the first refusal a visitor meets decides whether they believe the rest of it. Every message says what to do next rather than what went wrong.

FAQ, open and closed

The first answer is open on arrival and the rest are closed. The caret rotates on a spring, interruptible from wherever it is — somebody tapping three questions quickly must not watch three animations queue up behind each other.
