Owner forms

The few things the app can’t see for itself (§5): money moved between accounts, money the owner puts in or takes out, equipment and loans. Each form is a handful of fields and states what will change before it saves, account by account, with the before and after figures. No form mentions debit or credit (B-01). The last section adds Paid from to the existing expense form (F-1).

Moving money, and owner money

Banking the day’s cash is the most common entry in a shop’s books, so it is the first form. The owner-money form exists mainly to explain one thing: money the owner takes is not a cost of the shop.

The source account shows its balance right in the field, so “how much is there to bank?” is answered without leaving the form. Taking cash out of the drawer while a shift is open lowers that shift’s expected cash, as an expense from the drawer does (Q-15).
Before → after, per account, and then one sentence about profit. The sentence is the lesson: moving money and taking money are not costs. It is the most common confusion in small-shop books.
Managers see Move money only (Q-02). Owner money is the owner’s, and an admin records it on their behalf only with a note.
A transfer the statement already shows is matched, not doubled. Recording it here first just means the import finds it already there.

Equipment and loans

Buying a chest freezer and repaying the SACCO loan. These are the two forms where the app does sums the owner would otherwise get wrong: spreading a purchase over the years it lasts, and splitting a repayment into the loan and its interest.

Wear is explained in shillings per month, not as “depreciation”. The figure is cost ÷ years ÷ 12, straight line (§5). An accountant who uses another method changes it in one place, and later months follow.
The interest field can wait. Owners rarely have the lender’s split on the day. Blank posts the whole 6,000 against the loan and adds a Needs attention item, so profit is never quietly overstated.
On credit from a supplier adds a bill to that supplier (Phase H) instead of taking money now. My own money adds to the owner’s stake.
These are sample figures for April, after March’s figures on the other boards. Each frame starts from the same balances and is not added to the others.

What changes on forms that already exist

The expense form gains one field, Paid from (F-1), and it is required from the day the books start. A customer payment asks which account received it, but only when the shop has two accounts of that kind (D-12).

Paid from Cash drawer ties the expense to the open shift (F-5, Q-15). The hint says exactly what that changes at close. Without an open shift, the chip is replaced by Cash kept aside if the shop has that account, and is absent if it does not.
My own money records the expense and adds the same amount to the owner’s stake. This is how rent paid from a personal account gets into the books without a transfer.
Nothing else on either form moves. Field order, labels, the submit button and the offline message stay as they are today. The new field sits last before the button. NEW tags are board-only markers.
