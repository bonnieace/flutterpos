Statements

Three statements in plain words (B-01, §8): what the shop made (profit and loss), what it owns and owes (balance sheet) and where the money went (cash flow). Each plain name carries its accounting name in small type, for the accountant and the bank manager. The figures are Dev Corner Shop’s March 2027, the first month of its books. They come from one set of posted entries and agree with each other to the shilling.

Every line opens the entries behind it (shown hovered on Goods returned). Every entry opens its sale, return, bill or statement line. Nothing on this page is a dead end.
Section words are text, not strips (2026-10-13). An eyebrow with its accounting name, then indented lines, then a subtotal on a heavier rule. Profit is the only figure in mint.
Two things a shop owner should see here and doesn’t elsewhere: what the goods really cost after D-37’s adjustments (cost of goods sold includes them), and running costs as a share of sales. Profit is shown before tax, because tax is the accountant’s (§5).

On a phone, and the entries behind a figure

The same statement in one column, with the share of sales dropped. Then Bank and M-Pesa charges opened, and one of its entries opened in plain words.

The phone keeps the six largest running costs and folds the rest into one row that opens them. Lines are never cut off mid-figure.
An entry in plain words says which account went up or down and what was a cost. The same entry with debit and credit is one tap away, on the general ledger (B-01). It is not on this page.
“Posted by: the app” separates automatic entries from the owner’s forms and the accountant’s journals, which carry a name (B-04).

Customer money is one line with its three parts under it (D-36): deposits, credit and waiting money are owed to customers, never netted against what customers owe the shop.
Not sorted yet is shown, not hidden, with a glyph and its cause. A balance sheet that hides suspense looks finished when it isn’t. Sorting the line moves it to We owe suppliers, which drops to 37,760.
Worth to you fell by 2,822 in March even though the shop made 24,678. The owner took 30,000 and put in 2,500. The statement shows all three, so the owner sees why.

Where the money went

March’s cash flow, written the way an owner thinks about it: money at the start, what came in, what went out, money at the end. Moving money between the shop’s own accounts is left out, because it doesn’t change the total.

The answer to “I made 24,678, so where is it?” Money went up 9,345. Most of the gap is the 30,000 the owner took and the 5,100 of loan repaid. Against that, stock ran down by 8,990, and the shop owes suppliers 7,500 more. Each is on this page or the balance sheet, and the accountant’s export reconciles the rest line by line.
Running costs here are 74,650, not 82,682, because wear (1,417), the debt written off (450), and repairs the owner paid personally (2,500) moved no shop money. Interest (900) is counted with the loan repayment, and charges (2,315) and cash short (450) have their own lines. A note on the row says so when opened.
Direct method, in the owner’s categories. The accountant’s indirect cash flow (profit adjusted for changes in stock, debtors and creditors) is in the export (§9), not on this screen.
