1One sheet per list. surface.raised with the cardEdge line at radius.md, the box AppCard already draws, inside the 1200 records column. The filters, the rows and the paging are one object, so the page itself carries nothing but the toolbar.
2The bar. Search and filters on the left, the page’s one action on the right at its natural width. That replaces today’s full-width Add customer button, which is 1150 wide at this size. space.16 above and below and space.24 either side. These are the 48 controls the screen already has, with the same callbacks.
3A header that stays out of the way. labelEyebrow in text.tertiary (5.3:1), 40 tall, with border.strong beneath it. That is the one firm line in the sheet. A unit goes here once, in text.disabled, so every figure under it can drop its KES. Each header aligns the way its column does.
4The identity reads first. labelMedium white over a caption line in text.tertiary. Never more than those two lines, and no monogram tile: the initials repeat the name beside them, and the hue they were given meant nothing.
5Figures are a column, not prose. They use the mono at numericBody, aligned right and grouped in thousands, and they go white rather than mint. Whole amounts keep the developer’s no-.00 rule, with the .00 held as blank space so the digits still line up under 2,350.50. A missing value is an em dash in text.tertiary, never a zero.
6Status only by exception. A state the filter already implies, like Active here, is not drawn at all. A settled state is a glyph and a word in text colour. StatusBadge is kept for what someone should act on. So a column of badges becomes the two rows worth a look.
7Rows are 56 and the whole row opens the record. Hairline border.subtle between rows, no zebra and no vertical rules. Hover and press fill with surface.overlay, which keeps every ink at AA: tertiary 4.7:1 and problem red 4.5:1. A mint wash would have dropped tertiary to 4.35:1.
8Everything else is in the kebab. It has a 48 target, sits in the last column, and holds the same menu as today: View, Edit, Record a payment, Mark inactive. A row whose state wants one action gets that action promoted beside the kebab (see Cells and rows).
9The foot. The list’s own caption about its order on the left, and Pagination on the right. Paging is by cursor, so there is no “1–20 of 54” to show and none is drawn. The pager disappears when everything fits on one page, as it does today.

Six rules the sheet keeps

These rules are what make one component fit every list screen without each screen having to decide again.

Read across

One record, one line of sight

Everything a row says sits on its 56. A second line is allowed only under the identity or under a figure, as a caption. Text clips with an ellipsis; nothing wraps a row taller except a reason or note column marked two-line.

Monochrome body

Colour means state, and only state

The rows are white, sage and grey. Mint is kept for a total and for focus. Red, amber and green appear only inside a status, and always beside a glyph and a word. A sign is a real minus, so a loss reads without colour.

Units once

The header carries the unit

KES, UTC, units and % go in the header and not in every cell. Cells hold the number. The screen reader still hears the unit, because the row’s label is built from header and cell together.

Exceptions surface

Quiet when settled, loud when not

A settled state is drawn as a glyph and a word in text colour, or not at all. StatusBadge marks what wants attention. A row in that state may promote its one action out of the kebab, as Staff already does, but only where other rows do not need it.

Same data, same calls

A layout, never a behaviour

The rows, filters, paging, menus and role gates are the screen’s own. The sheet only decides where they sit. Where a board shows a figure the screen does not compute today, such as a total row, it is marked as an addition.

One component

Replaces three tables

RecordList’s table, the catalog’s and Staff’s each draw their own header, rules and figure style today, and two of them disagree on the header. The sheet is RecordList’s table grown up, so all three converge on it.
